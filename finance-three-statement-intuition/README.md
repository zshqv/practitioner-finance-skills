# finance:three-statement-intuition

## The Problem This Solves

Every financial modelling tutorial shows you how to fill in a three-statement model. They all show you the entries. None explain why the numbers move the way they do.

So you follow along, build the model correctly while watching the video, close the tab, open a blank spreadsheet — and immediately make the same errors:

- Adding depreciation instead of subtracting it
- Not understanding why net income and cash are different numbers
- Losing the thread when a concept transfers from one statement to another
- Building entries you cannot defend because you memorised them, not understood them

This is not a skill problem. It is a teaching problem.

## What This Skill Does

Makes Claude explain the three financial statements the way a 30-year practitioner explains them over coffee — not as an accounting exercise, but as a logical system where every entry makes complete sense once you understand what it is actually saying.

Covers:
- What each statement is actually asking before any numbers
- Why the balance sheet has to balance as a logical truth not a rule
- Why depreciation goes in three directions across three statements
- Why profitable companies go bankrupt
- How net income flows from P&L into retained earnings into equity
- Working capital and why it sits in the cash flow statement
- A mentor checklist to test your intuition before building any model

## The Origin

Built after hitting the exact wall it describes.

Learning three-statement modelling through BIWS and other tutorials. Could follow along and reproduce the model. The moment the tutorial closed and building from scratch began — constant errors. Adding depreciation in the wrong direction. Failing to understand why cash differed from profit. Losing the connection between statements the moment independent thinking was required.

The tutorials showed what. Nobody showed why.

After asking Claude to explain it and still finding the output too textbook — correct but not intuitive — this skill was built to force a different kind of explanation. One that builds the mental model first and lets the mechanics follow naturally.

## Why Claude Does Not Do This Well by Default

Claude's default explanations are technically accurate. They are also structured like a textbook — definitions first, mechanics second, examples third.

A practitioner mentor does the opposite. Intuition first. The aha moment before the formula. The analogy before the entry.

This skill restructures how Claude teaches finance — not what it knows, but how it explains it.

LLMs learn from text. Text teaches definitions and procedures. Mentors teach logic and consequences. Those are different educations.

## Who This Is For

- Self-taught finance learners hitting the wall between following tutorials and building independently
- Anyone who can reproduce a model but cannot explain why each entry goes where it does
- MFin and MBA applicants building financial modelling skills without institutional access
- Analysts early in their career who were never properly taught the intuition
